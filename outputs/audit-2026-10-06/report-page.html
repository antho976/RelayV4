RelayV4 Code Audit

Overview Themes All findings How it was done

RelayV4's foundations hold up. The single-store engine, the typed bus and one worktree per agent are sound, and the audit found no known-vulnerable dependency. The serious problems sit in four places. Guardrails can be walked around in ordinary ways. Slow work under the store lock can freeze every session. The phone door is exposed on the network without channel security. The desktop client rebuilds whole panels on every event.

Nothing stayed critical after verification. The one claimed critical, a confirmed hold landing in the wrong checkout, is real but needs uncommitted edits to the same file before it destroys work, so it is rated high.

    Scope

    The audit covered everything except the phone app:

    • the engine (relay-core) and the bus types (relay-bus);
    • the CLI and MCP servers, including the embedded Blender and Unreal Python;
    • the phone door;
    • the GTK desktop client;
    • tests, scripts, CI, plugins and docs.

    The audited commit was 361c6f9. Line numbers refer to the current main (d04b526), which is identical outside the 19 files that PR #30 and PR #31 changed.

    Round 1: find and verify

    • 475 agents at maximum effort, over about 19 hours.
    • 41 auditors each read one area in full.
    • 18 more each followed one concern across the whole codebase, such as the CLAUDE.md invariants, SQLite use, concurrency, security, data loss, GTK main-thread health, dead code, performance and misleading docs.
    • Every finding went to a verifier told to refute it, and every high one to a second skeptic.
    • Result: 1,126 candidates, 23 refuted.

    Round 2: consolidate and re-check

    • Duplicates merged. Two findings are one when a single fix resolves both, even when they cite different lines; a shared theme is not enough. 366 duplicates were folded into 197 groups, leaving 737 unique. A merged finding keeps every location and lists the reports it absorbed.
    • Blind re-check. Each of the 63 high or critical findings went to a fresh verifier that saw no earlier verdict or severity. It was told to refute by default and to rate severity anew. All 63 were confirmed. Seven were lowered to medium, and the one critical became high.
    • Completeness pass. Auditors' notes about issues they saw outside their area were mined for anything unreported. There were 21 such observations: 8 became verified findings and 13 were dropped.
    • Recheck after the newer merges. Ten findings sat near code that PR #30 or #31 edited. Six are still present, three changed and one is fixed. Other line numbers in those files were remapped through the diff.

    Medium and low findings had one adversarial verifier each, and no blind re-check.

    Severity

    Severity is judged for a single-user desktop app with a local engine, many concurrent agent sessions, and an optional phone door.

    Critical
    Data loss. A hole reachable by another local user, a network peer or an agent escaping its guardrails. An engine-wide hang.
    High
    Likely wrong behaviour in a normal flow, a bus or UI freeze, unbounded growth, or a safety check that is silently bypassed.
    Medium
    A real bug in a less common path, or a design problem with a concrete ongoing cost.
    Low
    A minor edge case or a modest cleanup.
